## The traced load's arrow

![The traced load, teal and haloed; the others pale](ui_v32_t12_traced_arrow.png)

While *Trace where one load goes* is on:

* the traced load's arrow is drawn in the trace's teal, thicker, on a
  pale halo, with a larger label;
* every other load fades to a pale tone;
* a small "traced" tag sits beside the arrow's shaft. The old "load
  traced from here" text sat on the shaft and was covered by the arrow.

When the trace is switched off, every load goes back to normal.

## The protocol, and the bridge it was tested on

**`LARGE_MODEL_PROTOCOL_v32_t12_2026-10-13.md`** is the procedure for a
truss of about a hundred members: plan, build in Excel, read the
stability line, analyse, declutter the canvas, check by hand, size the
families, report. Its example is a **64 m K-truss bridge** with 50
joints and 97 members, statically determinate. The tab agrees with every
hand check:

| | by hand | the tab |
|---|---|---|
| reactions | 450 kN | 450 kN |
| biggest chord force, M / d | 945 kN | 945 kN |
| end post | 450 kN | 450 kN |
| symmetry | 97 mirrored pairs | all agree |

`REPORTS AND GUIDES/large_model_protocol_v32_t12/` holds the Excel file
and the PDF, for the pinned bridge and for the same bridge with rigid
joints (21 sheets). The PDFs were written by importing the Excel file,
so reproducing them is: Import model from Excel, ▶ Analyze, Export PDF
report.

`apps/truss/truss_large.py` (new, Tk-free) generates the bridge for any
even number of panels, and works out the hand checks for the same
arguments.

## What the bridge showed was wrong, and is fixed

Running a hundred members through every step found these. None of them
changes the report of any truss in the example library; that was
checked, and none of them is slender or has a label hidden.

| Where | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Every drawing sheet | Arrows, supports, dimensions and margins were sized from the span. On a 64 m × 8 m bridge the load arrows were longer than the bridge was deep, and the margins left it 60 % of the sheet wide. | An **annotation scale**: the span for any truss up to 5 × as long as deep (so every existing sheet is unchanged), sized from the depth for a slender one. Side margins are capped too. The bridge is now 75 % of the sheet wide. |
| Geometry, Axial forces, Utilisation, Reactions, Joints | Every label was drawn where it fell. On the bridge the Axial forces sheet was a strip of overprinted digits. | Labels are placed **most important first**: the biggest forces, the worst utilisations, the largest joint moments. Any label that would overlap one already placed is left out, and the sheet says how many and where to read them ("40 of 112 labels left out…; every force is in the Rods table"). This is the rule the canvas has always used. |
| Reactions, Utilisation, Joints | Drawing beside the table, so a slender truss was a thin line in half the sheet | For a slender truss: drawing across the top, table or ranking underneath. The Joints table is split in two halves; the ranking shows the worst 10. |
| Utilisation | "The 18 most used of …" note sat on the bar chart's axis title | moved under it, right-aligned |
| Cover | 21 sheets listed one by one ran off the right edge | runs of one sheet are one entry: *Rod detail · 6 sheets* |
| Cover | `Family "Bottom chord"` ran into its own section | a *Rod families* line, then each name on its own |
| Every sheet | An imported model's report was titled "Truss" | named after the file it was imported from |

## Tests

* `tests/test_truss_large_model.py` (new, 16 tests) is the protocol as
  assertions:
  * size and determinacy, and an odd panel count refused;
  * solve time;
  * reactions, M / d, the end post and symmetry against the hand checks;
  * every family passing, and the deflection within L/300;
  * rigid joints changing the chords by under 1 %;
  * the slender drawing, at more than 70 % of the sheet width;
  * no two visible labels overlapping, and the note that counts the
    ones left out;
  * nothing in the band or the footer on any of the rigid report's 21
    sheets, and its contents fitting the cover;
  * the Excel round trip through the tab, the imported title, and
    tracing a load across the bridge (15/16 to the near support).
* `tests/test_truss_loadpath.py` gains one test: the traced arrow is
  teal, thicker and haloed; the others are pale; it follows the choice;
