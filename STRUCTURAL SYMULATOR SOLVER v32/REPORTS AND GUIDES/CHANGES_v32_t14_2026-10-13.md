## The view after ▶ Analyze

Analyze switches the deformed shape on, and on a long span, magnified,
it hangs well below the structure. At the same moment the diagram pane
opens underneath and the canvas gets shorter. The bridge's sagging chord
ended up under the force key.

Now, after ▶ Analyze:

* if the structure or its deformed shape runs out of view, including
  under the force key along the bottom edge, the view is brought back;
* **if it fits at your zoom, it is only panned into view.** It is
  zoomed out only when it does not fit. An early version zoomed a small
  Pratt *in* to fill the canvas; a test now pins that the Pratt's zoom
  is left alone;
* nothing moves when everything is already in view;
* nothing moves on a *Live* re-solve. That runs on every edit, and a
  view that jumps while you work is worse than one that needs Fit.

## Sheet 3: the labels

Looked at closely on the rigid bridge, the labels were already never
printed on each other, but:

* they butted edge to edge and sat on the joint markers;
* the 60 kN loads hang from the bottom chord, but their arrows were
  drawn coming down through the inside of the truss, lying exactly along
  each lower vertical. So none of those verticals ever got a label;
* fifteen identical "60.0 kN" labels sat where the diagonals cross;
* a label that collided at its member's midpoint was dropped, though
  there was often room a little further along, or just beside the
  member.

What the report does now, on every drawing sheet:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Room round every label** | a clear gap, and the joints count as taken: no label covers a joint |
| **Hung loads hang** | on the Reactions sheet (the only one that still draws loads, see below), a downward load whose arrow would pass through the truss is drawn hanging below its joint, the way a deck hangs from a through-truss; the inside of the truss stays clear |
| **Many equal loads, one label** | five or more identical loads get one note, "15 loads of 60.0 kN each, hung from the joints", instead of a label each |
| **Arrows count as taken** | a value is never printed across a load arrow (except the arrow's own label) |
| **Labels slide before they drop** | each label tries, in order: the middle of its member; just off the member on either side; further along it; the same text on two lines ("11" over "+719.4", about 40 % narrower). Only if none of these is free is it left out and counted. |

On the bridge's Axial forces sheet:

| | before | now |
|---|---|---|
| force labels shown | 57 of 97 (plus 15 load labels) | **83 of 97** |
| chord labels shown | 32 of 32, butting each other, then 0 of 32 in a first try of this round | **32 of 32**, clear of each other |
| lower verticals labelled | none (under the load arrows) | most (no load arrows on this sheet any more) |
| labels touching or covering a joint | many | **none** |

The library's small examples still show every label. That was checked
on all nine, and none hides anything.

## The load arrows

> *those black enormouse arrows are not didactic not fix anything please
> change them i dont want them in the final result. either delete them
> hide them or find another way to represent them because that is more
> confusing than no arrows at all.*

They are gone from the report's member sheets, and changed where they
stay:

* **Axial forces and Utilisation draw no load arrows.** Those sheets are
  about the members; the arrows only covered them.
* **The Reactions sheet keeps the loads**, because there they are half
  of the picture: the loads against the supports pushing back, and the
  sums in the table. Each one is now:
  * in the tab's own load **orange**, not black;
  * **thin**, with a small head;
  * **short**: at most half a typical member and 8 % of the drawing,
    where before it was 10 % of the drawing. On the bridge that is 2 m
    instead of 4 m, so no arrow reaches the next joint;
  * hung below the joint when the load hangs from it;
  * labelled once in a note when there are many equal loads.
* The Reactions caption says which is which: green reactions, orange
  loads.

The canvas on screen is unchanged; its loads were already orange and
sized to the screen.
